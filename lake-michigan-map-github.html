What's real here, and what isn't

Sample-site coordinates were pulled directly from your Google My Map ("PUMA - grant - GLFWRA"). Martin sighting clusters are real eBird occurrence records for Progne subis retrieved via the GBIF API (41.5–42.7°N, covering both the Illinois/Wisconsin shore, 87.2–88.2°W, and the Indiana/Michigan shore east to Benton Harbor, 86.2–87.3°W — roughly 3,000 of the most recent records across the two queries, clustered to ~1 km and filtered to clusters with 2+ reports; one boundary cluster common to both queries was deduplicated by keeping the higher count). The basemap is live vector map tiles (OpenFreeMap, built from OpenStreetMap data), so streets, place names, and the shoreline itself are the real thing — not a schematic.

PFAS layer detail

  • Naval Station Great Lakes (North Chicago, IL) — documented AFFF (aqueous film-forming foam) firefighting-foam use dating to the 1980s; listed among DoD installations under PFAS investigation. This is also one of your own sample sites.
  • Former Naval Air Station Glenview (Glenview, IL) — documented AFFF use for firefighting and seaplane operations, 1937–1995. Now a Navy/Illinois EPA Superfund cleanup; a 2023 PFAS groundwater test came back below MCLs.
  • Milwaukee Mitchell Air National Guard Base / Gen. Mitchell Field — documented AFFF source; PFAS-contaminated groundwater reported draining via the Kinnickinnic River into Lake Michigan. ~50 km north of your sample-site cluster.
  • Saukville Waterworks, WI (Ozaukee County) — an actual EPA UCMR5 (2023–2025) drinking-water sample: total PFAS 185.6 ppt in one sample (PFHxS 73, PFBS 29, PFOS 6.3, PFOA 4.2 ppt; other PFAS detected too). ~90 km north of your corridor.
  • Harbor Plating (Benton Harbor, MI) — not an AFFF site, a former chrome/cadmium plating operation. Real Michigan EGLE result: PFOS+PFOA up to 120 ppt in groundwater, above the state's 70 ppt standard. Approximate location from its street address.
  • Aircraft Components (D&L Sales) Superfund site and Kysor Industrial Corporation (both Benton Harbor, MI) — adjacent EPA-tracked industrial sites, PFAS detected 2021–2022, both approximately placed near the Paw Paw River.
  • Fort Sheridan (Lake Forest/Highwood, IL) — former U.S. Army installation with a documented Army Environmental Command PFAS Preliminary Assessment, tied to historical AFFF firefighting-foam use. Same location as your Fort Sheridan Forest Preserve site.
  • BP Whiting Refinery (Whiting, IN) — not a confirmed detection like the others on this list. Community and legal groups (incl. the University of Chicago's Abrams Environmental Law Clinic) flagged that the refinery's current discharge permit includes no PFAS limits or monitoring requirements, despite refineries' historical use of AFFF; regulators have not addressed whether PFAS is being discharged into Lake Michigan here. Flagged as an unaddressed risk, not a documented source.

Checked directly: no Illinois or Wisconsin utility within your actual sampling corridor (Milwaukee to the Illinois–Indiana line) shows a UCMR5-reportable detection in EWG's compiled dataset — including Chicago's own municipal system. That's a "clean at the tap" result for municipal drinking water, not an absence of PFAS in the environment; it doesn't cover industrial discharge, biosolids, or wildlife exposure pathways. EWG's own interactive map (ewg.org/interactive-maps/pfas_contamination) and EPA's PFAS Analytic Tools (echo.epa.gov/trends/pfas-tools) cover eleven data types total — industrial discharge, TRI releases, Superfund detections, and more — that aren't reproduced here.
